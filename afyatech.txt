# **AfyaTech (Kenya)** Integrated AI Strategy for African Healthcare*

# **Section 1: Diagnostic Report **

AfyaTech provides AI-assisted triage, SMS-based health education, and referral support for patients across Kenya and East Africa. A system audit across four domains reveals critical gaps:

---

## **1. Prompting Gap – AIM Framework Failure**

**Framework:** AIM (Audience–Intent–Mechanism)
**Failure:** Generic triage prompt: *“Assess patient symptoms and recommend next steps.”*

* No patient context (rural vs urban, literacy level)
* No clinical boundaries (advice vs diagnosis)

**Real-world harm:**

* Misclassification of conditions (e.g., malaria vs typhoid)
* Overconfidence → delayed hospital visits
* Unsafe recommendations in low-resource settings

---

## **2. Fluency Gap – 4D Framework Failure**

**Framework:** 4D (Directive–Data–Dialogue–Dynamic)
**Failure:** Static chatbot responses with no adaptive tone or local context

* Uses English-only clinical language
* No retrieval of Kenyan clinical guidelines

**Real-world harm:**

* Misunderstanding by patients using Swahili/Sheng
* Reduced adherence to treatment
* Loss of trust in digital healthcare tools

---

## **3. Ethics Gap – TRACK + OASIS Failure**

**Frameworks:** TRACK + OASIS
**Failure:** Patient phone numbers and symptoms stored raw on external cloud

* No anonymization
* No explicit consent for secondary use (AI training)

**Real-world harm:**

* Violation of **Kenya Data Protection Act (2019)**
* Risk of sensitive health data leaks (e.g., HIV status)
* Erosion of patient trust

---

## **4. Agent Gap – RANK + TRAIL Failure**

**Frameworks:** RANK + TRAIL
**Failure:** Single monolithic AI handles triage + referral + follow-up

* No clear authority boundaries
* No memory separation

**Real-world harm:**

* Conflicting outputs (e.g., “self-manage” vs “urgent referral”)
* Missed follow-ups for high-risk patients
* No human escalation pathway

---

# **Section 2: Redesigned AI System (≈1.5 pages)**

---

## 🧠 **1. Prompting Redesign (AIM + MAP)**

### **AIM Framework**

* **A – Audience:**
  “Kenyan patients using SMS/WhatsApp, varying literacy levels, possibly rural”

* **I – Intent:**
  “Provide safe triage guidance and recommend appropriate care level”

* **M – Mechanism:**
  “Stepwise symptom assessment + risk stratification + referral suggestion”

---

### **MAP Framework**

* **M – Mode:**
  SMS-friendly, simple language, bilingual (English + Swahili)

* **A – Anchoring:**
  Kenya Ministry of Health clinical triage guidelines

* **P – Precision (Final Prompt)**

> “You are a clinical triage assistant for Kenyan patients. Ask simple step-by-step questions in English or Swahili to assess symptoms. Do NOT diagnose. Classify risk into: home care, clinic visit, or emergency referral. If danger signs (e.g., difficulty breathing, unconsciousness) appear, immediately recommend nearest hospital. Ensure responses are clear for low-literacy users.”

---

## 💬 **2. Fluency Redesign (4D Framework)**

### **Interaction Redesign: Fever Case**

* **Directive:**
  Guide patient with fever through safe triage

* **Data (RAG Source):**
  Kenya Ministry of Health malaria guidelines + WHO fever protocols

* **Dialogue:**
  Adaptive tone:

  * Rural user → simple Swahili
  * Urban user → mixed English/Sheng

* **Dynamic:**
  Adjust response based on:

  * Age
  * Duration of fever
  * Presence of danger signs

---

### ⚙️ System Settings

* **Temperature:** 0.3 (low randomness for clinical safety)
* **Negative Prompt:**
  “Do NOT provide drug prescriptions, do NOT make definitive diagnoses, avoid medical jargon.”

---

## ⚖️ **3. Ethics Redesign (TRACK + OASIS)**

### **TRACK Audit Fix**

* **T – Training Data:**
  Include rural and informal patient datasets

* **R – Representation:**
  Balance across gender, geography, and socioeconomic status

* **A – Amplification:**
  Remove proxies like “hospital access” that bias against rural users

* **C – Counterfactuals:**
  Same symptoms → different location → same triage outcome

* **K – Kill Switch:**
  Pause system if triage disparity >10% across regions

---

### **OASIS Data Stewardship**

* **O – Opt-in:**
  Explicit SMS consent:
  “Reply YES to allow your data to improve AfyaTech services”

* **A – Anonymization:**
  Replace phone numbers with hashed IDs

* **S – Sovereignty:**
  Store data within Kenya-based or Africa-region servers

* **I – Intentional Retention:**
  Delete identifiable data after 6 months

* **S – Security:**
  End-to-end encryption for all patient interactions

👉 **Regulation:**
Aligned with **Kenya Data Protection Act (2019)**

---

## 🤖 **4. Agent Design (RANK + TRAIL)**

### **Agent 1: Triage Agent (RANK)**

* **Role:** Symptom assessment + risk classification
* **Authority Limits:**

  * Cannot prescribe medication
  * Cannot finalize diagnosis
* **Notification Trigger:**

  * Alert Referral Agent if emergency symptoms detected
* **Kill Switch:**

  * USSD *#911# to stop AI interaction

---

### **Agent 2: Referral Agent (TRAIL)**

* **Transient Memory:**
  Current patient case + urgency level

* **Relational Memory:**
  Nearby hospitals + patient opt-in location

* **Archival Memory:**
  Anonymized referral success rates

* **Inheritance:**
  Receives triage classification from Triage Agent

* **Land Rights:**
  Data stored in Africa-region servers only

---

### 🔁 **Handoff Flow**

1. Triage Agent detects emergency →
2. Sends [location, severity, symptoms] →
3. Referral Agent identifies nearest facility →
4. Suggests hospital + transport guidance →
5. Optional human nurse escalation

---

### 🛡️ Safety

* Block referrals based on biased factors (e.g., income level)

# **Section 4: Reflection (≈200 words)**

This course transformed my understanding of AI from a purely technical system into a **socio-ethical infrastructure embedded in African realities**. Initially, I approached AI as a tool for efficiency—faster triage, quicker outputs, scalable automation. However, the turning point came during the Ethical Savannah module, particularly when applying the TRACK and OASIS frameworks. I realized that even a “highly accurate” system could still produce harmful outcomes if it encoded historical bias or violated data sovereignty.

Crossing into the Agentic Pride deepened this shift. Designing multi-agent systems showed me that intelligence is not just about accuracy—it is about **coordination, accountability, and boundaries**. The idea that no single agent should dominate decision-making, especially in healthcare, reinforced the importance of human oversight.

The most profound shift was understanding that AI in Africa must be **context-aware, dignity-preserving, and sovereignty-respecting**. It is not enough to import global models; systems must reflect local languages, informal systems, and regulatory realities.

AI is no longer just a tool—it is a **public trust system**. And as a builder, my role is not just to optimize performance, but to **protect people, communities, and future generations**.
